Privacy Policy

For the app Day (“Day Calendar” on the App Store) and this site · Last updated: November 28, 2026

In short: Day has no account. Your events stay in the calendars you've set up on your device and never go to a server of ours. The app only downloads files from our server: release notes and, if you use them, holiday calendars. We don't track you. There are no analytics SDKs and no ads.

1. Who is responsible

Day is a private project by an individual and part of the aiity family. Send privacy questions to day@aiity.de; full provider details are in the legal notice on aiity.de.

2. No accounts, no trackers

There's no sign-up and no login. The app sends us no usage statistics, no telemetry and no automatic crash reports. Sections 9 and 10 describe when it downloads files from our server (aiity.de); it sends nothing about you or your events with those requests. No analytics or advertising SDKs are included, and no data is sold or shared. The app's privacy manifest accordingly declares no tracking and no collected data types.

3. Calendars and reminders

To show and edit your events, iOS asks for full access to your calendars. Day reads and writes events only through the calendars on your device (for example iCloud, Google or Exchange). Changes go straight to that calendar and are synced by its provider, whose privacy terms apply. Day doesn't copy events to a server of its own. Widgets, Live Activities and the Apple Watch app read the same calendars on each device. You can revoke access in iOS Settings at any time.

Some apps keep invisible identifiers in event notes. Day hides them and leaves them unchanged when you edit an event.

If you turn on Reminders in Day (optional), iOS asks for full access to your reminders. Day reads reminders that have a date and shows them in the calendar, and writes only what you do in Day, such as ticking one off or moving it. Day doesn't copy reminders to a server of its own either. Reminders you mark as urgent can ring like an alarm; iOS asks for permission for that, and the alarm is scheduled on your device. Which lists show as cards, your Day-only list groups and the list icons you pick are kept in Day on your device; they also follow your iCloud, as section 4 describes.

On the Mac you can turn on “Keep After Quitting Day” (optional). A small menu bar helper then starts at login and reads your calendars and reminders itself, read-only and only on your Mac. It has no network access.

4. Settings and iCloud

Your settings are stored on your device. When “Sync Settings with iCloud” is on (the default), Day stores selected settings, such as reminder times or your chosen maps app, in your Apple ID's iCloud key-value storage so they match on your other devices. Settings that follow include: which calendars and lists are shown and which are the defaults, how lists appear, your list groups and list icons (matched by account and name), favorites and templates, the holiday calendars you chose, the religion whose holidays you show, and your subscribed calendars with name, color and link. Such a link may contain a private access key. A choice of religious holidays can point to your religion; it stays on your device and in this iCloud storage and never reaches us. We have no access to this storage; Apple's privacy terms apply. Turn it off in Settings → General.

Photos you add to an event or reminder are picked with the system photo picker; Day receives only the chosen photo and has no access to your library. Day keeps a downsized copy on the device (also for the Days Counter widget) and, when “Sync Settings with iCloud” is on, in the app’s private iCloud storage in your Apple ID (its private CloudKit database, and its iCloud Drive area when iCloud Drive is available), so the photo appears on your other devices. This area isn’t visible in the Files app. Removing a photo deletes it on your other devices too. We have no access to this storage; Apple’s privacy terms apply.

5. Notifications

Event and leave-by reminders are scheduled by your device as local notifications. There's no push service and we hold no device tokens.

6. Location, places and travel times (optional)

If you use leave-by reminders, Day asks for your location while you use the app. Day then sends your approximate location and the event's place to Apple Maps to calculate the travel time. When you type a place, suggestions come from Apple Maps search; if you allowed location, Day uses it to show places near you. Apple handles these requests under its own terms; nothing reaches us. Day remembers found coordinates locally on your device.

7. Dictation (optional)

When you tap the microphone while adding an event, iOS asks for microphone and speech recognition access. If you've downloaded the Qwen3-ASR model (section 8), recognition can run with it on your device. Otherwise it is done by Apple's speech recognition, on the device where supported, otherwise on Apple's servers under Apple's terms. Day doesn't store recordings.

8. Apple Intelligence and on-device models (optional)

On devices that support Apple Intelligence, Day can recognise more details in what you type. This uses Apple's on-device language model.

Under Settings → On-Device AI you can also download open models (Qwen): one that reads what you type on your device without Apple Intelligence, and one for dictation (Qwen3-ASR). Nothing is downloaded until you ask. The models come from Hugging Face (huggingface.co) or, where that can't be reached, as in mainland China, from the mirror hf-mirror.com. The request goes straight from your device there; that provider sees your IP address and which model file you fetch, under its own terms. It never reaches us. What you type or say is processed by these models on your device and isn't sent anywhere. You can delete the models in Settings at any time.

9. Holidays and subscribed calendars (optional)

Holiday and school holiday calendars are downloaded from our server (aiity.de): the list of countries when you open the picker, and the file of a chosen country when you add it and about weekly after that, also in the background. The request names the country, not the region; it carries no cookies or identifier, and only “Day” as the program name. The server processes technically necessary connection data (IP address, time, requested file, user agent) to deliver and secure the files; the legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. Once you remove all holiday calendars, no more country files are downloaded.

Subscribed calendars: when you add a calendar link, Day downloads the file over HTTPS straight from that calendar's server: when you add it, when you open the app and the copy is over an hour old, now and then in the background, and when you refresh. Its provider sees your IP address, the time and the link; its privacy terms apply. The file stays on your device (also for the widgets) and is removed with the subscription. Nothing of this reaches us.

Religious holidays and the Chinese almanac are built into the app; nothing is downloaded or sent for them. The almanac is translated on your device.

10. Links, maps and sharing

Day opens meeting links, directions and websites in the apps you chose under Default Apps; their terms apply there. When you share an event, Day creates a calendar file that goes only where you send it.

When you open the release notes under Settings → What’s New, Day loads them from our server (aiity.de). If it can't be reached, the app uses GitHub Pages (enrzh.github.io) or the jsDelivr network (jsdelivr.net). The server in question processes the technically necessary connection data, such as your IP address, GitHub and jsDelivr under their own terms; Day sends no other data with it. Without a connection, Day shows the notes that came with the app.

11. Contacting us by email

If you write to us, we see your email address and your message. We use them only to reply, and delete them on request.

12. This website

This site is static, sets no cookies and loads no external fonts, scripts or analytics. When you visit, the server processes technically necessary connection data (IP address, time, requested file, user agent) to deliver and secure the site; the legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR.

13. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right of access (Art. 15), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction (Art. 18), data portability (Art. 20) and objection (Art. 21), and the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority (Art. 77). Content from the app never reaches us; when files are downloaded, our server processes only the connection data described above. Your events are in your calendars and your settings on your device and in your iCloud; you control both.

14. Changes

If the way the app works changes, this policy will be updated. The version published here, with the date above, applies.
